# The twelve-card objective deck

Every card is the same shape in different terrain: **three tiles you occupy in a
chain** — the middle tile touching both ends. Measured with seeking bots, two
per batch, 58–116 samples each.

| # | Objective | Chain | Achieved | 95% interval |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Foothills | mountain – forest – plains | 64.7% | 56–73 |
| 2 | Watershed | mountain – plains – ocean | 64.7% | 56–73 |
| 3 | Highland Rivers | mountain – forest – ocean | 53.4% | 44–63 |
| 4 | Fjord | mountain – ocean – mountain | 51.7% | 39–65 |
| 5 | Mountain Pass | plains – mountain – plains | 48.3% | 35–61 |
| 6 | Coastal Chain | plains – ocean – plains | 40.5% | 32–49 |
| 7 | Clearing | forest – plains – forest | 39.7% | 27–52 |
| 8 | **Mountain Lookout** | ocean – mountain – ocean | 37.9% | 29–47 |
| 9 | Riverbank | plains – forest – ocean | 37.9% | 25–50 |
| 10 | Timberline | forest – mountain – forest | 36.2% | 27–45 |
| 11 | River Delta | ocean – plains – forest | 34.5% | 22–47 |
| 12 | Sheltered Water | forest – ocean – forest | 29.3% | 18–41 |

**Mountain Lookout lands at 37.9%**, mid-deck — a good addition rather than a
outlier in either direction. It is the mirror of the Fjord: Fjord puts the ocean
between two mountains, the Lookout puts the mountain between two oceans, and the
Lookout is the harder of the two (37.9% against 51.7%) because ocean tiles are
scarcer on the map than mountain.

**Deck size.** Twelve is enough for four players to be dealt two each with four
cards still unseen, so no table ever sees the whole deck. That was the point of
expanding it.

**Spread.** 29.3% to 64.7%, a ratio of 2.2×. That is wider than I would choose
if the global rate were what players compared — but with deal-two-keep-one, the
decision is which chain fits the hand you drafted, and hand composition moves a
card's difficulty far more than these averages do. Worth watching at a table:
**Foothills and Watershed are the two easiest** and could be dropped, or moved
to 3 points, if they turn out to be auto-keeps.

**Why every card is a chain.** A chain of three terrains needs about one tile of
each, which is exactly what a per-suit explore budget of roughly two supports.
Single-terrain patterns need three of one suit and hit that ceiling — they
measured 10–47%, and the high end only because the three-player starting map is
itself a mountain triangle, seeding those objectives rather than requiring them
to be built.
