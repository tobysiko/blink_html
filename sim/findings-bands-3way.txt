WHY EQUAL BANDS ARE THE WORST OF THE FOUR
-----------------------------------------
A meld places ONE UNIT PER CARD, so a big meld consumes the very band that
permits it:

    layout     units at limit 4   units at limit 5   most 4-melds   most 5-melds
    4/6/8/2           8                  2                2              0
    5/5/5/5           5                  5                1              1
    3/6/7/4           7                  4                1              0
    2/4/6/8           6                  8                1              1

Across a whole game a player places 20 units. If every meld were five cards
they would play four melds all game. The ceiling on big melds is structural.
Time at limit 4 is worth more than time at limit 5: hands fill a four-card
meld far more often, and it costs 20% of the reserve rather than 25%.

5/5/5/5 pays three units out of the limit-4 band to buy time at limit 5, and
loses a third of its four-card melds doing it.

WHERE 3/6/7/4 LANDS
-------------------
It is the conservative option and it behaves like one. Against the current
bands it more than doubles five-card melds (0.05 -> 0.12) and adds a quarter
to four-card melds (1.48 -> 1.79), for +3.2 gold of upkeep. It changes the
opening least of the three alternatives - Founding only drops from 4 to 3, so
the gentle first rounds survive.

But 0.12 five-card melds per player per game is still one every eight games.
The straight of five and the full house remain effectively absent. 3/6/7/4
improves the four-card tier; it does not rescue the fifth rung.

Note the score split moves in an interesting direction: 50/32/18, the highest
victory-row share of any layout tested. Fewer units placed per turn than
2/4/6/8 leaves more gold for upgrades.
