Without A the LEADER is slightly favoured, which is the tie-break doing its
job: "if still tied, whoever played earlier wins". Turning A on inverts it.
The last player sees every meld already on the table and can spend exactly
enough to take the trick; the leader has no information and my bot never
declares A from that seat at all (0.0% of its melds).

Final scores stay close (24.6 / 26.2 / 23.8) because the trick winner LEADS
the next round - winning puts you in the worst seat for A, which is negative
feedback. The advantage oscillates rather than compounding.
